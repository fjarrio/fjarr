| 28 | **A bulk transfer over the tunnel stalls in roughly half of attempts.** **Where it stands after slice 4.5e's follow-up (2026-09-27).** One cause is fixed and measured A/B: the packet pump fell off the end of its `-> bool` fd-watch callback, and the double unref that followed aborted the agent inside glibc's allocator seconds into any saturating transfer (3 of 3 pre-fix, 0 of 3 fixed; `-Werror=return-type` and `NetCapability.aSaturatedBatchKeepsTheWatchAndLosesNoPacket` keep it out). **Every arm measured before that fix is contaminated by it** — the crash could fail any arm — so the reliable-ordered (2 of 8), bounded-loop (7 of 20) and watermark arms in the 4.5c review are not evidence either way and have to be re-run. **The abandonment is now counted**: `link-stats` carries `abandoned` and `abandoned_error`, from a log hook on GStreamer's `sctpassociation` category at ERROR level (the event is log-only in gst-plugins-bad 1.28 — no signal, no state change), and opsim prints the robot's number on every per-second line. Five gate runs with it watching, on a robot restarted onto that build: 3 green, 2 red — the band the gate was already in, so the instrument does not change the outcome. **Abandonment does not predict failure**: 281 abandonments in a pass, 0 in another pass, 71 and 171 in the two failures. **A failure is the whole association wedging**, permanently and in both directions — tx and rx frozen at +0 for 20 s and more, the control channel's `close` unanswered, no further `link-stats` — while ICE and DTLS stay up and the agent answers on its loop (261 of 261 introspect polls in ≤1.4 ms straight through two failing runs, measured in 4.5e). The two failures stalled at 89 % and at 99.9 % of the transfer; earlier ones at ~40 MB and ~100 MB. `abandoned_error` reads 0 on every event. Also known: the fault is rate-sensitive — `GST_DEBUG=sctp*:3` suppresses it (8 of 8), which is why the counter uses level 1 — and `fjarr-connect` (webrtc-rs, its own SCTP) has moved 1 GiB 5 times for 5 with no video beside it. Ruled out with a clean measurement: core-loop starvation, transfer size, the MTU check, the operator's device writes, the 4 MiB watermark, video sharing the connection *as a throughput matter* (#23) | [docs/27](27-network-tunnel.md#testing), [slice-4.5c review](reviews/slice-4.5c-review.md), [slice-4.5e review](reviews/slice-4.5e-review.md) | before the M4.5 gate | **The video-plus-`fjarr-connect` arm has run: 5 of 5 green** — 1 GiB hash-verified at 244-275 Mbps beside a viewer at a steady 30 fps, with 22 / 151 / 58 / 132 / 418 abandonments per run that the transfer did not care about. With opsim + video at about half red, `fjarr-connect` alone 5 of 5 and `fjarr-connect` + video 5 of 5, the traffic pattern and the video load are both cleared, and what differs is the far end: opsim is webrtcbin over usrsctp, `fjarr-connect` is webrtc-rs over its own SCTP. **A usrsctp-to-usrsctp association wedges; a usrsctp-to-webrtc-rs one has not, in ten runs.** That reframes the question: the failing operator is a lab tool, and neither real operator (`fjarr-connect`, or the browser on Chromium's dcsctp) is usrsctp. It does not close it — the robot's usrsctp sits on the wedged association too, and which end stops first is unknown. Next: a wedged opsim run with the association state read on **both** ends (usrsctp's `sctp_getassocinfo` / flight-size and cwnd, via the `sctpassociation` object's debug output at the moment of the wedge), which says whether this is opsim's bug or the robot's; and the reliable-ordered arm again, post-fix, since a PR-SCTP abandonment leaving usrsctp's flight-size accounting wrong is the one mechanism that fits a permanent bidirectional wedge triggered more often at higher rates. If it is the robot's, a fix that is pacing, batching or a second association is a wire or design change and wants an ADR; if it is opsim's, the gate moves to `fjarr-connect` and opsim's tunnel scenario is demoted to a smoke check |
